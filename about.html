Planning & design process

Common Ground is a community resource website for health and human services. Our goal: a resident with a phone and a few spare minutes can find the right service, see what it costs, and know how to reach it.

Our process, step by step

  1. Research. We reviewed community health needs data and existing 211-style directories to learn which services residents look for most and where current sites fall short.
  2. Audiences and tasks. We defined three people to design for: a parent looking for affordable child care, an older adult who needs rides to appointments, and a teen looking for someone to talk to. Each became a test task.
  3. Site map and wireframes. We kept navigation to five destinations (Home, Browse resources, Events, Get help, About) and sketched every page at phone size first.
  4. Visual system. We chose a warm, friendly look so the site feels welcoming rather than clinical: a cream background, a berry primary color, leaf-green and sky-blue accents, a classic serif for headings, and friendly illustrated characters.
  5. Build. We hand-coded the site in HTML, CSS, and JavaScript with no frameworks or internet connection required. One stylesheet and one script serve every page, so the header, footer, type, and colors stay consistent.
  6. Test and refine. We checked each page at desktop, tablet, and phone widths, tested keyboard-only navigation, verified color contrast, and confirmed there are no console errors.

Key design decisions

  • Search first. The home page puts a large search bar directly under the headline, because most visitors arrive with a specific need.
  • Two ways to find help. Visitors who know what they want use the filterable directory. Visitors who feel unsure use the three-step guided flow, which leads to the same filtered results.
  • Plain language. Categories use everyday words (“Food security”, “Jobs & child care”), and every listing shows hours and cost.
  • Crisis help on every page. The 988 and 911 numbers sit in the same spot on every page.

Accessibility statement

Common Ground is designed to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1 at Level AA. If anything on this site is hard to use, contact us at hello@commonground.example or (555) 555-0100.

Features we implemented

  • Semantic HTML landmarks (header, nav, main, footer) and one logical heading outline per page.
  • A “Skip to main content” link as the first item on every page.
  • A visible 3-pixel focus outline on every link, button, and form control, never removed.
  • Every control works with a keyboard alone, including filters, bookmarks, the calendar, and the guided flow.
  • Visible labels on every form field; filter groups use fieldsets and legends.
  • Result counts, bookmark changes, and calendar updates are announced to screen readers through live status messages.
  • Toggle buttons (dark mode, larger text, bookmarks, calendar view) report their on/off state with aria-pressed.
  • A “Larger text” setting enlarges all text by about 19%, and layouts reflow without horizontal scrolling at 200% browser zoom.
  • A dark theme with its own separately checked contrast ratios.
  • Information is never shown by color alone: categories are labeled in text, and selected chips show a checkmark.
  • Descriptive alternative text on every image, and decorative illustrations are hidden from screen readers.
  • Animation is limited to the small hero characters and turns off when a device is set to reduce motion.
  • Buttons and links are at least 40 pixels tall for easier tapping.

Known limitations

  • Phone links open a calling app only on devices that support calling.
  • Settings and saved resources last only for the current browser session by design.

Compatibility notes

The site uses only standard HTML, CSS, and JavaScript. It needs no internet connection, no installation, and no web fonts, so it runs straight from the project folder on a laptop.
